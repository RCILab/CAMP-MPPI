Overview

Physical feasibility should be enforced inside the rollout.

Contact-rich manipulation is constrained not only by where the robot moves, but also by the interaction wrench required to realize that motion.

A kinematically valid sample can still be physically inadmissible if the required contact, support, or grasp wrench exceeds its feasible set. CAMP-MPPI maps these heterogeneous physical constraints into a common commanded-acceleration space, composes them into one admissible set, and projects every sampled control to its closest feasible acceleration under the generalized inertia metric.

Motivation

Contact consistency and wrench admissibility are coupled.

In surface interaction, the end-effector must remain consistent with the contact geometry while sustaining an admissible contact wrench. A force limit used only as a soft rollout penalty does not make the sampled control itself physically feasible.

What this work contributes

01

One admissible space

Dynamics-level contact, support, and grasp wrench constraints are pulled back into commanded-acceleration inequalities and composed with acceleration-level kinematic constraints.

02

Inertia-weighted joint projection

Every raw MPPI proposal is minimally corrected by one projection under the generalized inertia metric, rather than by separate sequential corrections.

03

Feasible executed command

At the current receding-horizon step, all projected samples lie in the same convex admissible set, so their MPPI-weighted average remains admissible.

Method

Compose. Project. Propagate.

CAMP-MPPI turns wrench and kinematic feasibility into sample-wise admissibility before trajectory-cost reweighting.

1

Map physical constraints

The interaction wrench required by a commanded acceleration is affine at a fixed rollout state. Wrench-set membership can therefore be represented as linear inequalities on the commanded acceleration, in the same space as the kinematic constraints.

2

Project each sample

The inequalities are stacked into a single convex admissible set. Each raw MPPI sample is projected to the closest feasible acceleration using the generalized inertia matrix as the metric.

3

Roll out on the manifold

When persistent contact is present, the projected command is propagated through manifold-constrained dynamics. MPPI then scores and reweights the resulting full-horizon rollouts.

Experimental settings

Two hardware tasks, two kinds of physical limits.

The paper evaluates CAMP-MPPI on a Franka Research 3 manipulator with GPU-parallel MPPI rollouts, using tasks that stress coupled kinematic and dynamics-level feasibility under distinct physical interaction regimes.

A

Surface writing under contact-manifold and wrench constraints

The end-effector follows a prescribed surface while maintaining persistent contact and satisfying admissible interaction-wrench bounds.

B

Passive-object transport with obstacle and support-wrench constraints

A tall passive object is carried on a tray through an obstacle-constrained environment. The controller needs enough evasive motion to avoid collision without inducing a support wrench large enough to tip the object.
