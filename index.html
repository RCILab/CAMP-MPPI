Overview

Physical feasibility should be enforced inside the rollout.

Contact-rich manipulation is constrained not only by where the robot moves, but by the interaction wrench required to realize that motion. A kinematically valid sample can still be physically inadmissible if the required contact, support, or grasp wrench exceeds the feasible set.

CAMP-MPPI addresses this by expressing heterogeneous physical constraints in a common commanded-acceleration space. At each rollout state, it rebuilds one composite admissible set, projects every sampled command into that set under the generalized inertia metric, and then propagates the projected command through manifold-consistent dynamics when persistent contact is present.

Motivation

Contact consistency and wrench admissibility are coupled.

In surface interaction, the end-effector must remain consistent with the contact geometry while sustaining a physically admissible wrench. Treating force limits only as soft penalties can still allow samples that rely on slipping, losing contact, or destabilizing the manipulated object.

What this work contributes

01

One admissible space

Contact-, support-, and grasp-wrench constraints are mapped into commanded-acceleration inequalities and composed with acceleration-level kinematic safety constraints.

02

Inertia-weighted projection

Each raw MPPI proposal is minimally corrected by a joint projection under the generalized inertia metric, rather than through separate sequential fixes.

03

Feasible executed command

At the current receding-horizon step, all samples share the same convex admissible set, so the MPPI-weighted average of projected first-step commands remains feasible.

Method

Compose. Project. Propagate.

CAMP-MPPI turns wrench and kinematic feasibility into sample-wise admissibility before trajectory cost reweighting.

1

Map physical constraints

The wrench required by a commanded acceleration is affine at a fixed rollout state. Wrench-set membership can therefore be pulled back into linear inequalities on the commanded acceleration. Kinematic safety constraints are expressed in the same space.

2

Project each sample

The heterogeneous inequalities are stacked into a single convex admissible set. Every raw MPPI sample is projected to the closest feasible acceleration using the generalized inertia matrix as the metric.

3

Roll out on the manifold

When persistent contact is present, the same projected command is propagated through manifold-constrained dynamics. MPPI then evaluates full-horizon costs and reweights the feasible sampled rollouts in the usual receding-horizon loop.

Experimental settings

Two hardware tasks, two kinds of physical limits.

The paper evaluates CAMP-MPPI on a Franka Research 3 manipulator with GPU-parallel MPPI rollouts. The experiments are designed to stress coupled kinematic and dynamics-level feasibility under distinct interaction regimes.

A

Surface writing under contact-manifold and wrench constraints

The end-effector follows a prescribed surface while maintaining persistent contact and satisfying admissible interaction-wrench bounds. This setting tests manifold consistency and dynamics-level contact feasibility together.

B

Passive-object transport with obstacle and support-wrench constraints

A tall passive object is carried on a tray through an obstacle-constrained environment. Successful motion requires enough evasive acceleration to avoid collision without demanding a support wrench large enough to tip the object.
